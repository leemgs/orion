\label{sec:introduction}

When model weights, activations and key--value (KV) caches exceed accelerator
memory, inference latency is governed less by arithmetic than by how execution
coordinates data movement across device memory, host memory and
storage~\cite{ma2026inference,gholami2024memorywall}. Because inference
dominates the recurring cost and energy of deployed models, this coordination
has direct economic and environmental
stakes~\cite{iea2024electricity,stojkovic2025dynamollm,juniewicz2026hyperscaler},
and it recurs across autoregressive language
models~\cite{grattafiori2024llama,jiang2024mixtral}, vision and
vision--language models~\cite{dosovitskiy2021vit,li2023blip2} and
retrieval-augmented pipelines~\cite{johnson2019faiss}. Hardware analyses
identify memory capacity, bandwidth and interconnect latency as governing
constraints~\cite{zhou2024survey,li2024llm,miao2025towards}.

Systems relieve this pressure along many axes. Serving frameworks manage the KV
cache and schedule
requests~\cite{kwon2023pagedattention,yu2022orca,du2025prefillonly,jin2024p};
offloading and memory-pooling systems spill weights or activations to host
memory or
storage~\cite{flexgen2023,aminabadi2022deepspeedinference,xu2024pie,neo2024,2025specoffload,ellm2023};
swapping systems stage tensors on and off the
accelerator~\cite{huang2020swapadvisor,wang2024swapnet,kida2025revisiting};
sparsity, pruning and IO-aware attention shrink the working set or its
traffic~\cite{frantar2023sparsegpt,dao2022flashattention,korthikanti2023reducing};
parallelism redistributes memory across
devices~\cite{huang2019gpipe,shoeybi2019megatronlm}; and host allocators and
NUMA-aware placement shape the sustained transfer
path~\cite{osdi21malloc,yang2023numalloc,milic2017beyond,waldspurger2002ballooning}.
The design space has prompted several
surveys~\cite{long2025survey,wang2020convergence} and widens further on edge and
heterogeneous
hardware~\cite{sarkar2023edge,luo2025toward,he2023cpu,beaumont2020optimal}. Yet
the same technique can help or hurt depending on model size, batch size,
hardware topology and how much compulsory traffic remains on the critical path,
and published comparisons rarely report enough to tell which condition applied.

Classical tools each capture one side of this problem. Working-set
analysis~\cite{mattson1970evaluation} treats capacity saturation on a single
tier, and the Roofline model~\cite{williams2009roofline} bounds performance by
$\min(F \cdot I,\, B_{\mathrm{peak}})$ for a fixed workload on a single tier.
Neither alone distinguishes two operating points with the same arithmetic
intensity and working-set size when one can overlap its cross-tier traffic and
the other cannot, whereas queueing descriptions of overlapping
services~\cite{kleinrock1975queueing} represent overlap but supply no portable
coordinate for residency. We address this narrow gap with a common coordinate
system for a multi-tier hierarchy, not a replacement for these frameworks.

We make three contributions. First, an identifiable operating coordinate:
independently measurable residency $R_C$ and overlap $R_B$ separate capacity
pressure from exposed transfer without using total latency to explain itself.
Second, bounds that map diagnosis to intervention: a conservative service-time
bound yields a minimum non-resident share $\max(0,1-R_C)$ and an unhideable
transfer share $\max(0,1-R_B)$; only $R_B=1$ is a derived crossover, whereas
$R_C=0.5$ is a declared convention whose sensitivity must be reported. Third, a
falsifiable open protocol with bounded evidence: a compiled processor probe and
layered-matrix runs on \NumAccel{} accelerators of two vendors test the
predicted directions. The evidence establishes directional consistency, not
production effect sizes, policy rankings or per-device boundary values.
